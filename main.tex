\title{Momentum Regime Shifts: A Bayesian Hidden Markov Model Approach to Cross-Sectional Stock Selection}
\author{Gilad Gang}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Cross-sectional momentum is one of the most robust return anomalies in empirical finance, yet it is subject to severe crashes during market stress episodes. This thesis investigates whether a real-time regime signal can improve long-short momentum through \emph{stock selection} -- changing which stocks are held -- rather than the \emph{exposure scaling} approach of Daniel and Moskowitz (2016), and whether this mechanism requires nonlinear modelling. A two-stage framework combines a Bayesian Hidden Markov Model with machine learning stock selection. In the first stage, a two-state HMM estimated via Gibbs sampling on four macrofinancial stress indicators (drawdown, return dispersion, relative market participation, and credit spread) produces a filtered probability of market panic. In the second stage, three portfolio construction methods of increasing complexity -- a deterministic formula, logistic regression, and XGBoost -- combine this regime signal with momentum at 12 lookback horizons and fundamental stock characteristics to rank stocks into a long-short portfolio each month. Out-of-sample evaluation on 2011--2025 U.S. equities (167 months) reveals a stark finding: in long-short construction, only the nonlinear model (XGBoost) produces economically meaningful returns, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.97 and highly significant annualised factor model alphas ($t > 3.2$, $p < 0.001$) that are unexplained by standard risk factors. All other strategies collapse, including logistic regression (Sharpe 0.05) despite having identical input features. The regime signal is essential for this result: it enables XGBoost to switch between momentum continuation in calm markets and short-term reversal during panic, exploiting a regime-dependent rotation in momentum's term structure that model-free information coefficient analysis confirms. This rotation is invisible to a linear model because the regime signal functions as a context variable -- governing which momentum horizons are informative -- rather than a direct return predictor. The strategy's vulnerability lies in prolonged bear markets where reversal bets fail beyond one month, suggesting a potential hybrid with exposure-scaling circuit breakers as a direction for future work.
\end{abstract}
