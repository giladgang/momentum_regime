\title{Momentum Regime Shifts: A Bayesian Hidden Markov Model Approach to Cross-Sectional Stock Selection}
\author{Gilad Gang}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Cross-sectional momentum is commonly treated as a single anomaly, yet its behaviour varies substantially across market conditions. This thesis investigates how a real-time regime signal interacts with the cross-sectional momentum anomaly and what this interaction reveals about momentum's underlying structure. A two-stage framework combines a Bayesian Hidden Markov Model, estimated via Gibbs sampling on four macrofinancial stress indicators (drawdown, return dispersion, relative market participation, and credit spread), with machine learning stock selection models. Three methods of increasing complexity -- a deterministic formula, logistic regression, and XGBoost -- combine this regime signal with momentum at 12 lookback horizons to rank stocks into long-short portfolios each month. Out-of-sample evaluation on 2011--2025 U.S. equities (167 months) reveals that only the nonlinear model (XGBoost) produces economically meaningful long-short returns, while the linear model collapses despite having identical input features. Model-free information coefficient analysis confirms the mechanism: momentum is not a single stable signal but a family of horizon-specific effects whose composition rotates with market conditions. In calm markets, intermediate-horizon continuation dominates; in panic, short-horizon reversal takes over. The regime signal functions as a \emph{context variable} -- it does not predict returns directly but governs which momentum horizons are informative in a given state. This nonlinear interaction is captured by XGBoost's tree splits but is invisible to a linear model. The findings offer a complementary perspective to the exposure-scaling approach of Daniel and Moskowitz (2016): rather than adjusting how much of a fixed portfolio to hold, the regime signal can be used to adjust which stocks to hold. Stress tests identify the strategy's vulnerability to prolonged fundamental deterioration, where the short-term reversal mechanism breaks down.
\end{abstract}
