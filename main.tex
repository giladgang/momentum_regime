\section*{Management Summary}

Cross-sectional momentum, the tendency for recent stock-market winners to keep winning and losers to keep losing, is one of the most profitable anomalies in finance and one of the most fragile. Momentum portfolios suffered losses exceeding 50\% during the 2009 market rebound and crash repeatedly around regime transitions. This thesis examines what form cross-sectional momentum's regime-dependence takes, and how a nonlinear model captures and exploits that form. The aim is to understand the mechanism, not to propose a strategy that outperforms the market on aggregate.

\paragraph{Introduction (Section~\ref{sec:introduction})} The thesis introduces a real-time regime signal derived from a Bayesian Hidden Markov Model applied to four macrofinancial stress indicators. The signal enters a cross-sectional stock-selection model as a context variable that conditions how the model jointly uses the full momentum term structure, not as a direct return predictor. The thesis asks what form cross-sectional momentum's regime-dependence takes, and what mechanism drives it.

\paragraph{Literature (Section~\ref{sec:literature})} The thesis sits at the intersection of four literatures. Standard cross-sectional momentum at intermediate horizons \citep{JegadeeshTitman1993, NovyMarx2012}, with behavioural drivers in gradual information diffusion \citep{HongStein1999}; momentum crashes and exposure-based risk management, where \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016} and \citet{BarrosoSantaClara2015} reduce gross exposure during stress; regime-conditional asset pricing, where \citet{Cooper2004} and \citet{GHM2023} use binary or weight-blending regime treatments; and machine learning in cross-sectional return prediction \citep{Gu2020}. The thesis investigates how these strands interact, characterising the mechanism by which the cross-sectional structure of momentum reorganises across regimes when a continuous probabilistic regime signal and the full momentum term structure are jointly fed to a nonlinear cross-sectional model.

\paragraph{Methodology (Section~\ref{sec:methodology})} A two-stage framework. Stage one estimates a Bayesian two-state Hidden Markov Model via Gibbs sampling on four monthly stress indicators, producing a filtered probability of market panic. Stage two combines this regime signal with twelve momentum horizons in models of increasing complexity (a deterministic formula, a logistic regression, and an XGBoost ensemble), evaluated in long-short construction with NYSE breakpoints and value weighting.

\paragraph{Data (Section~\ref{sec:data})} The sample is U.S. equities (CRSP, 1990--2025), split into a 1990--2010 training period and a 2011--2025 out-of-sample test period (167 months). Delisting returns are incorporated following \citet{Shumway2001} to prevent survivorship bias: actual delisting returns are used when available, and a $-30\%$ return is imputed for performance-related delistings with missing returns. The HMM feature set is chosen via a four-pass selection pipeline (quality screen, portfolio ranking, definitive validation at higher seed counts, and seed-stability check). International replication uses Compustat Global for the United Kingdom and Japan, with the HMM feature set re-selected per region.

\paragraph{Results (Section~\ref{sec:results})} XGBoost achieves an out-of-sample Sharpe of $1.11$ and a six-factor alpha of $24.1\%$ ($t = 4.81$); the regime-conditional advantage is concentrated in panic, where XGB earns $1.53$ Sharpe against $0.84$ in calm and the long-short momentum benchmarks collapse or stay near zero. Removing the regime signal cuts XGB's Sharpe by more than $60\%$, establishing that the signal is essential. The linear baseline on the same features collapses, establishing that the regime-momentum interaction is inherently nonlinear. Cluster analysis of the long leg's z-curve profiles recovers four recurring selection modes that trace a single market cycle (classical winner selection in calm, hedged selection in transition, dispersed-recovery loser selection in post-panic months, and term-structure-wide reversal in active panic). Cluster-level CAPM betas confirm that XGB avoids the leg-beta inversion that drives traditional momentum's crashes. International replication produces Sharpes of $0.84$ in the United Kingdom and $0.50$ in Japan with locally fitted HMMs.

\paragraph{Conclusion (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion})} The thesis makes two contributions to the momentum literature. First, the methodological combination is itself novel: prior regime-conditional momentum work pairs simple regime treatments (binary indicators, volatility thresholds, fast-slow blends) with linear or rank-based rules, whereas this thesis pairs a continuous probabilistic HMM regime filter built from multiple stress indicators with a nonlinear cross-sectional model (XGBoost), and the combination is what enables the empirical mechanism. Second, the analysis of that mechanism reveals two interlocking facets of a regime-conditional mechanism: structurally, XGB ranks stocks on predicted forward return rather than per-horizon momentum rank, which allows the long leg to hold stocks not top-ranked at any individual horizon, a composition that no rank-based momentum strategy can produce by construction; this compositional flexibility behaviourally enables the inversion of selection direction in panic while keeping the long-short portfolio fully invested, in contrast to the exposure-cutting responses of \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016} and \citet{BarrosoSantaClara2015}. Limitations include the 167-month test sample, specification search on the HMM feature set, and reversal-mode vulnerability to prolonged fundamental deterioration. Future work should extend the framework to longer historical windows that include depression-style downturns and to additional asset classes.
