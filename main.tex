\title{Momentum Regime Shifts: A Bayesian Hidden Markov Model Approach to Cross-Sectional Stock Selection}
\author{Gilad Gang}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Cross-sectional momentum is one of the most robust return anomalies in empirical finance, yet its strength varies substantially across market conditions. This thesis investigates how a real-time regime signal interacts with the momentum anomaly and what this interaction reveals about momentum's underlying structure. A two-stage framework combines a Bayesian Hidden Markov Model with machine learning stock selection. In the first stage, a two-state HMM estimated via Gibbs sampling on four equity market stress indicators (drawdown, realised volatility, return dispersion, and relative market participation) produces a filtered probability of the market being in a panic state. In the second stage, three portfolio construction methods of increasing complexity -- a deterministic formula, logistic regression, and XGBoost -- combine this regime signal with momentum at 12 lookback horizons and fundamental stock characteristics to rank stocks each month. The logistic regression tests whether a linear model can exploit regime information; XGBoost tests whether nonlinear regime-momentum interactions matter. Out-of-sample evaluation on 2011--2025 U.S. equities (167 months) provides evidence that the regime-conditioned models deliver meaningfully higher risk-adjusted returns than unconditional momentum and existing market-state approaches. Decomposition analysis suggests that fundamental quality screening (profitability, valuation, size) is the primary driver of risk-adjusted improvement, while the regime signal contributes differently: it does not improve Sharpe ratios beyond what a linear model achieves, but it enables XGBoost to generate higher absolute returns through regime-adaptive stock selection. The central finding is that the regime signal is not a direct return predictor; it is a context variable that governs which momentum horizons are informative. Intermediate-horizon momentum (8--11 months) dominates in calm markets, while 1-month reversal becomes the relevant signal during panic. This interaction is invisible to the logistic regression but is captured by XGBoost through tree splits. A risk-aversion analysis provides further evidence that the regime signal is most valuable when the investor is willing to bear risk, as its relative SHAP importance drops from nearly 30\% under return maximisation to approximately 6\% under high risk aversion.
\end{abstract}
