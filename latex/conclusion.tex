\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The central finding of this thesis is that, in the sample studied, momentum's cross-sectional predictability shifts with market conditions; which lookback horizons carry predictive power, and in which direction, depends on the prevailing market regime. The regime signal captures this shift not by predicting returns directly but by conditioning how the model jointly uses the full momentum term structure, a multi-dimensional interaction that only a nonlinear model can exploit.

In out-of-sample evaluation on 167 months of US equities (2011--2025), XGBoost trained on the twelve momentum horizons together with the HMM filtered probability $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ achieves a Sharpe ratio of 1.11 with a six-factor alpha of $24.1\%$ ($t = 4.81$). Both linear baselines on identical features collapse (LR Sharpe $-0.01$, Ridge $-0.57$), establishing that the regime-momentum interaction is inherently nonlinear and not an artefact of the training target. Removing the regime signal cuts XGB's Sharpe to $0.43$, a $60\%$ drop. Cluster analysis of the model's picks recovers four recurring selection modes (calm continuation, transition, post-panic recovery, active panic) with cluster Sharpes from $0.92$ to $1.82$; the economic value is concentrated in the panic-side reversal modes (clusters 3 and 4), which cover only $31\%$ of the test sample but produce the cluster Sharpes that pull the overall result well above unconditional momentum's $0.06$. International replication on UK and Japanese equities, with locally-fitted HMMs, produces Sharpes of $0.84$ and $0.50$ respectively, indicating that the mechanism transfers across markets.

The expanding-window backtest extending the OOS evaluation to 25 years (2000--2024) confirms that the strategy survives the historical stress events outside the main test sample. The 2009 momentum crash, which produces a $-62.6\%$ loss for unconditional momentum, generates $+20.5\%$ for the regime-conditional strategy. The dot-com bust nevertheless produces the strategy's largest historical drawdown ($-64.8\%$), the empirical realisation of the reversal-failure mode discussed in the limitations below. In calm and bull sub-periods the market dominates on Sharpe (the long-short construction adds short-leg volatility without comparable upside in trending markets), while XGB outperforms in stress periods. The strategy is therefore a stress-period complement to, not a substitute for, market exposure.

These findings complement the exposure-scaling approach of \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016} and \citet{BarrosoSantaClara2015} along two distinct channels. First, the HMM filtered probability provides a continuous, smoothed regime signal, a probabilistic alternative to the binary bear-market indicators used to gate exposure-scaling rules. Second, the cross-sectional model uses that signal to reorganise which stocks fill each leg rather than to scale gross exposure; the regime signal conditions the direction of momentum selection, and the leg-beta inversion that drives traditional momentum's crashes is absent in the resulting portfolio (Appendix~\ref{app:leg_betas}). The two approaches are complementary, and a hybrid that adds D\&M-style exposure scaling on top of XGB would address the reversal-failure vulnerability without sacrificing XGB's stock-selection advantage in shorter panics.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:contributions}

The two contributions outlined in Section~\ref{sec:introduction} are supported by the empirical evidence. The routing-variable mechanism that produces the two reversal modes is documented by the cross-sectional z-score profiles (cluster 3 below the cross-section average while the cross-section is up; cluster 4 uniformly below average across all 12 horizons in a falling market) and by the cluster-level SHAP shares, which show $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ contributing more when the regime call is ambiguous and less when it is clear. The level-versus-rank selection is established by the cluster-level CAPM betas, which show XGB's long-leg $\beta$ above its short-leg $\beta$ in every cluster while fixed 12-month momentum exhibits the inversion that drives crashes after bear markets. Together, these findings suggest that the fragility of momentum documented in prior work may partly reflect unconditional models averaging over a regime-dependent structure, rather than an intrinsic deficiency of the anomaly itself.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Economic vulnerability}
The strategy's primary vulnerability is a prolonged economic deterioration where beaten-down stocks do not recover because their underlying businesses are failing rather than because of temporary mispricing. Section~\ref{sec:stress_test} discusses this reversal-failure mode and the complementary D\&M exposure-scaling overlay that would mitigate it. Calm-period continuation provides a floor, but a sustained downturn where reversal consistently fails would be painful.

\paragraph{Historical realisation of the vulnerability}
The strategy's largest historical drawdown is the dot-com bust ($-64.8\%$ peak-to-trough; $-43.8\%$ cumulative over the 32-month bear-market proper, March 2000--October 2002, under expanding-window OOS; see Section~\ref{sec:historical_stress}). This is the empirical realisation of the reversal-failure vulnerability described above. A sustained multi-year decline without any reversal (a depression-style outcome) lies outside the 25-year historical sample and would require a D\&M-style exposure-scaling overlay to manage.

\paragraph{Test period scope}
The test period (2011--2025, 167 months) does not contain a prolonged recession. The 25-year expanding-window backtest (Section~\ref{sec:historical_stress}) confirms that XGB would have survived the 2009 momentum crash, but the dot-com result there is the historical realisation of the reversal-failure vulnerability discussed above. Performance is strongest in 2021--2025 (Sharpe 1.70), a period with frequent regime transitions; in calm-dominated years the model earns modest returns from standard continuation. A permanently calm market would reduce the strategy to ordinary momentum.

\paragraph{Frozen HMM parameters and regime drift}
The HMM is estimated on 1990--2010 and held fixed during the test period. This assumes that the statistical properties of calm and panic regimes remain stationary. If the market undergoes structural change (a permanent increase in baseline volatility due to algorithmic trading or shifting monetary policy regimes, for example), the trained thresholds may become miscalibrated, with structurally calm periods classified as panic. The sustained elevation of $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ in recent years may partly reflect such drift rather than genuine prolonged stress. The 25-year expanding-window backtest in Section~\ref{sec:historical_stress} (in which the HMM is re-estimated annually using only data available at the time) reproduces the production result on the 2011--2024 overlap, indicating that the regime structure is reasonably stable; online re-estimation would still provide a stronger guarantee.

\paragraph{Memoryless regime conditioning}
XGBoost observes only the current month's features and has no memory of the $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ trajectory, so a fresh spike and a sustained elevation are treated identically despite their different implications for reversal. Section~\ref{sec:future_work} sketches a sequential extension.

\paragraph{Implementation frictions}
The analysis assumes frictionless short selling and applies a uniform 10 basis point one-way transaction cost. Cost sensitivity (Appendix~\ref{app:transaction_costs}) confirms XGB remains profitable at 50 bps, but the uniform cost understates the borrow fees and hard-to-borrow constraints that would apply most strongly to the short leg for small or less liquid stocks. Value-weighting and NYSE decile breakpoints tilt the portfolio toward large, liquid names, partially mitigating this concern.

\paragraph{Specification search}
The HMM feature selection procedure uses out-of-sample portfolio Sharpe in its second pass, meaning the selected combination (DD+DISP+REL\_N+CS) is informed by test-period outcomes. Section~\ref{sec:feature_selection} discusses the mitigating factors in full (the quality screen in Pass~1, the cross-seed stability criterion in Pass~3, and the qualitatively similar performance of leave-one-out specifications, Table~\ref{tab:hmm_feature_ablation}); but the reported 1.11 Sharpe should be read with this caveat.

\paragraph{Sample scope and causal inference}
The sample is restricted to US equities. The interpretation that the regime signal conditions momentum's term structure is supported by multiple lines of evidence (SHAP, tree paths, ablation, LR-XGB divergence) but is inferred from observational data and should be read as consistent evidence rather than definitive proof. The Newey--West $t$-statistic on XGB's excess return over the market is 4.37\textsuperscript{***}, well above conventional thresholds, and the factor-model alphas in Appendix~\ref{app:factor_alphas} confirm this strength across all five specifications (CAPM, FF3, Carhart, FF5, FF6: every $t > 4$).

\subsection{Future Work}\label{sec:future_work}

Four directions extend this framework most directly.

\paragraph{Richer regime-signal representation}
The current cross-sectional model conditions only on the contemporaneous filtered probability $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$, which discards information about regime dynamics. Two complementary extensions are natural. Looking forward, the HMM's transition probabilities support a $k$-step regime forecast $P(s_{t+k} = 1 \mid \mathbf{z}_{1:t})$, which would let the portfolio reposition before a regime shift rather than reacting to it; the expected gain is concentrated in transition months, where the current framework trades on a stale view of the regime in precisely the months when the portfolio is furthest from its regime-conditional optimum. Looking backward, the trajectory of $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ distinguishes a fresh spike (where mean reversion is most likely) from a sustained elevation (where the reversal mechanism may be breaking down), but the current scalar input collapses these into the same value. A sequential model, such as an LSTM, a transformer over a fixed window of regime features, or feature engineering with lagged $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ values, a short-window rate of change, and a duration-since-entry counter, would expose the trajectory directly. The two extensions are complementary; the forward direction provides anticipation, the backward direction provides memory.

\paragraph{Alternative nonlinear models}
XGBoost is one of several nonlinear model classes, and the central mechanism (that $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ gates the use of momentum horizons) should in principle be recoverable by any sufficiently expressive architecture. The Random Forest comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:xgb_sensitivity} partially tests this; bagging still selects $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ as a material feature, but the drop in Sharpe (from $1.08$ to $0.73$) and the fall in its SHAP share (from $45\%$ to $18\%$) show that the boosted additive structure is what elevates the regime signal from a marginal predictor to a primary gating variable. The more informative test is whether the mechanism survives under genuinely different inductive biases. An attention-based model would learn horizon-by-regime weights directly and produce an interpretable per-horizon attention profile that could be compared against the SHAP decomposition in Table~\ref{tab:shap}; a regularised multilayer perceptron with explicit $\pi \times mom_k$ and $mom_k \times mom_j$ interaction inputs would isolate whether the dependence on boosting reflects sequential residual fitting or a shortage of data for richer architectures. Recovering the same mechanism across these alternatives would strengthen the claim that the regime-conditional shift is a property of the data rather than of XGBoost specifically.

\paragraph{Regime-conditional feature sets}
Adding ten firm-level fundamentals to XGB reduces Sharpe from $1.11$ to $0.89$ (Section~\ref{sec:fund_ablation}) because the new features absorb $44\%$ of SHAP importance and dilute the regime-momentum signal. The fund-only variant attains a calm Sharpe of $0.86$ (matching the baseline's $0.84$) but a panic Sharpe of only $0.38$, because firm-level characteristics describe stable attributes that do not flip direction when the regime shifts. The optimal feature set may itself be regime-dependent. During panic, when reversal dominates, momentum and the regime signal carry the mechanism. During calm, when the continuation premium is weaker and more diffuse, fundamentals may provide a useful second dimension. A layered construction in which XGB produces the initial long-short ranking and fundamentals enter as a post-ranking filter (excluding bottom-quintile-quality names from the long leg, high-leverage names from the short leg) is a natural starting point; a more ambitious variant trains separate cross-sectional models for the calm and panic branches, each with its own feature set. Both designs require careful out-of-sample validation because the regime-conditional split reduces effective sample size.

\paragraph{Exposure-scaling overlay (D\&M complement)}
The strategy's primary vulnerability, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, is a prolonged economic deterioration where beaten-down stocks do not recover and the reversal mechanism fails. XGB's stock-selection channel does not address this directly; its contribution is to choose which stocks to hold, not how much. The two channels are complementary. A natural extension is to combine XGB with a \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016}-style exposure-scaling overlay that reduces gross exposure when a sustained-stress indicator is triggered, for example when $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ has been above a threshold for a minimum number of consecutive months, or when its rolling maximum over a fixed window exceeds a calibrated bound. Such a hybrid would reduce the risk of a total collapse during a prolonged bear market without sacrificing XGB's stock-selection advantage in shorter panics. Preliminary checks on the 2011--2025 sample show no improvement from the overlay because the sample contains no prolonged recession, so the circuit breaker only cuts profitable panic months; its value would emerge in a sustained downturn outside the current window. The dot-com sub-period of the 25-year expanding-window backtest (Section~\ref{sec:historical_stress}) is a natural setting to evaluate candidate overlay rules.
