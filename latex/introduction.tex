\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

This thesis applies machine learning to study the behaviour of cross-sectional momentum across market regimes. The setting is the U.S. equity cross-section from January 1990 to November 2025, with international replication in the United Kingdom and Japan. The central object of study is the cross-sectional momentum anomaly, the tendency for recent stock-market winners to continue outperforming and losers to continue underperforming \citep{JegadeeshTitman1993}. The aim is to better understand this anomaly conditional on the market regime, not to propose a strategy that outperforms the market on aggregate.

Cross-sectional momentum is one of the most robust anomalies in empirical finance and one of the most fragile. Momentum portfolios suffered losses exceeding 50\% during the 2009 market rebound \citep{DanielMoskowitz2016}, and similar crashes have recurred around regime transitions. The fragility motivates a substantial literature on momentum-crash management, but existing responses share a structural assumption. The underlying selection rule (winners minus losers, ranked) is treated as unconditional, and only the rule's exposure or weighting is adjusted around regime transitions. \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016} and \citet{BarrosoSantaClara2015} scale gross exposure during stress; \citet{Cooper2004} treat regimes as a binary up-or-down variable; \citet{GHM2023} blend fast and slow horizon weights. What this leaves unexamined is how the full momentum term structure and the regime signal jointly play out, that is, the mechanism by which the cross-sectional structure of momentum reorganises across regimes.

This thesis examines that possibility. The central claim is that cross-sectional momentum's behaviour differs systematically across regimes, with the regime signal serving as a \emph{context variable} that exposes the difference. The signal carries no direct return-predictive content but conditions how the cross-sectional model jointly uses the full momentum term structure in a given market state. No prior study has documented this regime-dependent structure directly, or shown that capturing it requires nonlinear modelling.

\paragraph{Research question} The thesis asks how the cross-sectional structure of momentum changes between calm and panic regimes, and what mechanism drives that change.

\paragraph{Approach} The question is answered in three steps. First, a Bayesian Hidden Markov Model is estimated on four monthly macrofinancial stress indicators (drawdown, return dispersion, relative market participation, and the BAA-AAA credit spread), producing a filtered probability of market panic that updates monthly. This regime signal is combined with the twelve momentum horizons in cross-sectional stock-selection models of increasing complexity (a deterministic formula, a logistic regression, and an XGBoost ensemble), each evaluated out-of-sample on 2011--2025 U.S. equities in long-short construction. Second, the chosen model's selection rule is opened up month by month with SHAP decomposition and a clustering of long-leg cross-sectional z-curves, identifying the role the regime signal plays in selection. Third, the strategy is tested outside the main test sample, on a 25-year expanding-window backtest covering the dot-com bust, the GFC, and the 2009 momentum crash, and on UK and Japanese equities with locally-fitted HMMs.

\paragraph{Headline findings} Cross-sectional momentum's structure reorganises systematically across regimes through a single mechanism: in panic, the regime signal flips the model's selection direction while keeping the long-short portfolio fully invested, in contrast to the exposure-scaling responses of \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016} and \citet{BarrosoSantaClara2015}; cluster-level CAPM betas show that the leg-beta inversion that drives traditional momentum's crashes is absent in every regime. The model's long leg systematically holds stocks that are not top-ranked at any individual momentum horizon, sitting below the cross-section average at every horizon during recovery and active crisis, a regime-conditional pattern that rank-based momentum strategies cannot produce by construction. Capturing this reorganisation requires both a probabilistic regime signal and a nonlinear model: on 2011--2025 U.S. equities, the XGBoost ensemble on $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ and the twelve momentum horizons produces a six-factor alpha of $24.1\%$ ($t = 4.81$), the linear baseline on identical features collapses, and removing $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$ alone cuts XGBoost's Sharpe by more than $60\%$. Cluster analysis of the model's picks recovers four recurring selection modes that trace a single market cycle (calm-period winner selection, a hedged transition, and two distinct panic-side reversal modes during recovery and active crisis), and the same reorganisation appears on UK and Japanese equities with locally fitted HMMs.

\paragraph{Contributions} The thesis makes two contributions to the momentum literature. First, the methodological combination is itself novel: prior regime-conditional momentum work pairs simple regime treatments (binary up-or-down indicators, single-variable volatility thresholds, or fast-slow blending weights) with linear or rank-based cross-sectional rules, whereas this thesis pairs a continuous probabilistic regime filter built from multiple stress indicators (the Bayesian HMM filtered probability $\pi_t^{\text{filter}}$) with a nonlinear cross-sectional model (XGBoost), and the combination is what enables the empirical mechanism. Second, opening up the model's behaviour cluster by cluster (Section~\ref{sec:novelty}) yields two patterns that the prior momentum literature does not document: a regime-conditional inversion of selection direction that operates at the composition level rather than the exposure level, which complements rather than substitutes for the exposure-scaling crash-management literature of \citet{DanielMoskowitz2016} and \citet{BarrosoSantaClara2015}; and a long-leg composition that no rank-based momentum rule at any single horizon could reproduce, extending the multi-horizon \citep{NovyMarx2012} and multi-asset \citep{Asness2013} momentum literatures into a regime-conditional setting.

\paragraph{Outline} The remainder of the thesis is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:literature} (Literature Review) reviews the relevant literature on momentum's horizon structure, momentum crashes and exposure-based risk management, regime models in asset pricing, and machine learning in cross-sectional finance. Section~\ref{sec:methodology} (Methodology) develops the two-stage framework, including the Bayesian HMM specification and Gibbs sampling estimation, and the cross-sectional stock-selection methods. Section~\ref{sec:data} (Data) describes the U.S. equity sample, the four-pass HMM feature selection pipeline, and the cross-sectional features. Section~\ref{sec:results} (Results) presents the out-of-sample results, the cross-sectional z-score and cluster analysis that documents the regime-momentum mechanism, robustness and risk-aversion analyses, and international replication. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} (Summary, Conclusions and Recommendations) summarises the contributions, discusses limitations, and suggests directions for future research.
